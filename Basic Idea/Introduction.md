## 🧠 What’s Social Engineering?

**Social engineering** is basically hacking people, not computers. Instead of brute-forcing passwords or exploiting software bugs, you’re playing **mind games**. You’re using human feelings—like kindness, fear, greed, or even curiosity—against them.

It’s like a psychological Jedi mind trick:

> “This _is_ the email you’re looking for.”

---

## 🎣 Enter Phishing: The Digital Bait & Hook

Now imagine social engineering, but delivered via **email**. That’s **phishing**. It’s like someone casting a net and hoping to catch anything that bites.

Phishing emails might:

- Look like they’re from your boss, a bank, or a service you use (like Netflix or Microsoft)
    
- Ask you to click a link or download something
    
- Lead to a fake site that steals your login info
    
- Or even run malware on your machine behind the scenes
    

In short: it’s all about tricking the user to open the gates for the attacker.

---

## 🗡 Spear-Phishing: Targeted and Deadly

Now take that net and turn it into a **spear**—you’re aiming for _one_ person or organization, not just anyone.

A **spear-phishing** email is:

- Custom-crafted
    
- Looks hyper-legit (maybe it mentions your name, your boss, or your favorite cookie shop)
    
- WAY harder to detect because it’s not generic spam
    

This is a **Red Team’s secret weapon** during an engagement. You’re testing how well an organization can detect and defend against a **realistic, focused attack**.

Why it’s scary good?

- Personalized = more believable
    
- Spam filters and antivirus struggle to block it
    
- Once you’re in, you could pivot and spread
    

---

## ☎️ Beyond Email: Other Phishing Flavors

- **Smishing** = Phishing via SMS/text messages (think: “Hey it’s HR, click here to verify your info”)
    
- **Vishing** = Voice phishing, aka scam calls pretending to be tech support, banks, or even the FBI.
    

They’re all cousins in the **phishing family**—same con, different disguise.

## 🍪 Example Breakdown: The Cookie Trap

Let’s unpack the Ultimate Cookies scenario:

1. **Attacker finds the company’s location** – physical proximity adds credibility.
    
2. **Discovers a _real_ local cookie business** – makes it feel legit.
    
3. **Registers a lookalike domain** – `ultimate-cookies.thm` (note the ".thm" instead of ".com").
    
4. **Sends a tasty bait email** – “Free cookies if you sign up!”
    
5. **Victim signs up using their company email + reused password** – uh-oh.
    
6. **Attacker now has access to their email** – time to snoop and move laterally.
    

That’s how one “free cookie” turns into **company-wide compromise**.

---

## 👨‍💻 Red Team Perspective

A red team might:

- Run a phishing test to **see who bites**
    
- Use the access to escalate further inside the network
    
- Deliver a report showing **human vulnerabilities**, not just tech flaws
    

It’s like a cybersecurity **secret shopper** mission—but instead of rating service, you’re poking holes in their defense.
