
---

### 1. **Detection & Identification**

- **Alert**: Get notified about the phishing attack, either through tools or user reports.
    
- **Investigate**: Look at the email for signs of phishing: strange sender, urgent language, suspicious links, or attachments.
    

### 2. **Containment**

- **Block the Sender**: Block the phishing email’s sender and domain.
    
- **Delete/Quarantine Emails**: Remove or isolate the phishing emails from all mailboxes.
    
- **Block Malicious Links**: Block any harmful URLs linked in the email.
    
- **Block Harmful Attachments**: Stop malicious files from running.
    

### 3. **Eradication**

- **Remove Malware**: If malware was delivered, clean it from infected machines.
    
- **Reset Passwords**: Change any stolen credentials immediately.
    
- **Update Defenses**: Strengthen email filters and endpoint protection to prevent the same attack.
    

### 4. **Recovery**

- **Restore Systems**: Get any infected systems back to a clean, safe state.
    
- **Monitor**: Keep an eye out for any further phishing or attacks.
    

### 5. **Post-Incident Analysis**

- **Analyze**: Investigate how the attack happened and what vulnerabilities were exploited.
    
- **Educate Users**: Teach users who fell for the phishing attack to be more vigilant in the future.
    
- **Update Plans**: Improve your incident response plans based on what you learned.
    

### 6. **Reporting**

- **Report Internally**: Document the steps you took and report to leadership.
    
- **Report Externally**: Notify customers or authorities if sensitive data was compromised.
    

### 7. **Lessons Learned & Improvement**

- **Review the Attack**: Look back on the incident to see what worked and what could be improved.
    
- **Update Procedures**: Make changes to your security policies and procedures to prevent future phishing attempts.
